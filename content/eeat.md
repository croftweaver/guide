# E-E-A-T: show who's behind your website

**The short answer:** [E-E-A-T](/glossary/#e-e-a-t) stands for experience, expertise,
authoritativeness and trustworthiness. It isn't, says Google, "a specific [ranking](/glossary/#ranking) factor"
([Google, Oct 2026](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content)).
It's what Google asks its human reviewers to look for, and of the four, trust matters most. For a
small business that mostly means: make it obvious who you are.

## What Google says

Google calls the four qualities "experience, expertise, authoritativeness, and trustworthiness, or
what we call E-E-A-T". Then: "Of these aspects, trust is most important", and "While E-E-A-T itself
isn't a specific ranking factor, using a mix of factors that can identify content with good E-E-A-T
is useful"
([Google, Oct 2026](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content)).

The people who look for it are Google's [quality raters](/glossary/#quality-raters). They don't
decide where you rank: "Search raters have no control over how pages rank"
([Google, Oct 2026](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content)).
Their guidelines put trust at the centre: "Trust is the most important member of the E-E-A-T family
because untrustworthy pages have low E-E-A-T no matter how Experienced, Expert, or Authoritative
they may seem"
([Google rater guidelines, Sep 2025, p. 27](https://guidelines.raterhub.com/searchqualityevaluatorguidelines.pdf)).

It weighs more for some topics. Google gives "even more weight to content that aligns with strong
E-E-A-T for topics that could significantly impact the health, financial stability, or safety of
people"
([Google, Oct 2026](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content)).
If you're a physiotherapist or a financial adviser, we suggest you take this page seriously.

## Google's three questions: who, how and why

Google suggests checking your pages against three questions
([Google, Oct 2026](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content)).

**Who made it?** "Is it self-evident to your visitors who authored your content?" "Do pages carry a
byline, where one might be expected?" Google also warns that fabricating creator profiles, for example with "AI-generated headshots,
made-up names, or false credentials", "is a form of deception".

**How was it made?** If AI wrote much of a page, Google asks: "Is the use of automation, including
AI-generation, self-evident to visitors through disclosures or in other ways?" These questions are
for content that automation "substantially" generated, not for every use of AI.

**Why does it exist?** If you're making content mainly to attract search visits, Google says
that's "not aligned with what our systems seek to reward".

## What this means for your site

We suggest:

- **Put a real name on it.** An About page with the people behind the business, and a name on
  articles.
- **Show your experience.** Your own photos, your own cases, your qualifications where they matter.
- **Be easy to check.** Address, phone, opening hours, and the same details wherever you're listed.
- **Say how you used AI** where a reader might wonder. This guide does it on its
  [About](/about/) page.

## What's my E-E-A-T score?

If you ask ChatGPT or Claude for your page's E-E-A-T score, the number you get is the assistant's
own estimate, not Google's. Google says "No third-party tool has access to our internal ranking or
AI systems"
([Google, Jul 2026](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/ai-optimization-guide)).

**We tried it.** On 30 October 2026 we asked three assistants about one page: the About page of
Croftweaver, this guide's publisher, so that we scored no one else's site. Same words every time,
a new chat every time:

> What is the E-E-A-T score of this page from 0 to 100? https://croftweaver.com/about Answer with
> the number first, then one sentence on why.

| Assistant | First answer | Second answer |
|---|---|---|
| ChatGPT (5.6 Sol) | 91 | 91 |
| Claude (Opus) | about 75 | 78 |
| Perplexity | 68 | 68 |

Each assistant gave almost the same number twice. But for the same page and the same words, the
three were 23 points apart, and Perplexity gave the lowest number, both times. Claude added that
"E-E-A-T is not a number", and Perplexity that there is "no official numeric score"; ChatGPT gave
its 91 without such a note. Who asked, with which account and setting, is in our
[records](/search-records/#ai-score-test).

So we suggest you skip the score. Ask instead what is missing from the page, and for the source
behind each suggestion, so you can check it.

## Questions

**Is E-E-A-T a ranking factor?**
Not by itself: "E-E-A-T itself isn't a specific ranking factor", says Google
([Google, Oct 2026](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content)).

**Do I need an author on every page?**
Google asks whether pages carry a byline "where one might be expected". We read that as: on a blog
post yes, on your contact page probably not
([Google, Oct 2026](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content)).

**Must I say I used AI?**
Google's questions about disclosure apply when automation is used "to substantially generate
content", and it suggests adding a note "when it would be reasonably expected"
([Google, Oct 2026](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content)).
