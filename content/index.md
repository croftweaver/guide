# Get found on Google and in AI answers

A free guide for people who run a website and have better things to do than learn jargon.

It's written so that anyone can follow it. When a technical word can't be avoided, it links to the
[glossary](/glossary/), where it's explained in everyday language. And every time we say how Google
or an AI service behaves, we link the page that says so. Where we found no published answer, we
tell you that too, and show you what we searched.

## Three things that get a website found

**1. Let them in.** Google reads websites with programs called
[crawlers](/glossary/#crawler)
([Google, Dec 2025](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/how-search-works)), and so
do AI companies such as OpenAI ([OpenAI](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/bots)). Turn them
away and some services say plainly that you won't be shown in their answers: sites that block OpenAI's search crawler
"will not be shown in ChatGPT search answers", though they "can still appear as navigational links"
([OpenAI](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/bots)). → [AI crawlers](/ai-crawlers/)

**2. Say clearly what you do.** Google's AI answers are built on its normal search: the best
practices for [SEO](/glossary/#seo) "continue to be relevant", because these features are "rooted in our
core Search [ranking](/glossary/#ranking) and quality systems"
([Google, Jul 2026](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/ai-optimization-guide)). So we
suggest you start where you always would: clear pages about what you do, where, and for whom. Work
aimed at AI answers has its own names, such as [GEO](/glossary/#geo). → [SEO basics](/seo/),
[GEO](/geo/)

**3. Show who you are.** Google asks its reviewers to look for experience, expertise,
authoritativeness and trustworthiness, which it calls [E-E-A-T](/glossary/#e-e-a-t). Of the four, "trust is
most important"
([Google, Oct 2026](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content)). →
[E-E-A-T](/eeat/)

## How this guide keeps itself honest

- **Every claim has a source.** When we say what Google or an AI does, the link goes to the page
  that says so, and the date beside it is the date that page gives.
- **We say when we found no source.** Then you'll read *we found no published …*, with a link to
  what we searched and when.
- **Our opinions are marked as advice.** You'll recognise them by "we suggest".
- **Every page shows when we last checked it.** Mistakes go into a public list of corrections on
  the [About](/about/) page.

## Questions

**Is SEO dead now that AI answers questions?**
Not according to Google. Asked whether SEO is still relevant for AI search, its own guide answers:
"In short, yes!"
([Google, Jul 2026](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/ai-optimization-guide)).

**Do I need special tricks for AI?**
For Google, no. Its guide lists things you can ignore, among them llms.txt files and "Rewriting
content just for AI systems"
([Google, Jul 2026](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/ai-optimization-guide)). For the
other AI services, each page of this guide says what each company publishes.

**Who writes this?**
